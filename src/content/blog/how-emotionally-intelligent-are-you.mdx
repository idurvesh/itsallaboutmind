Arjun noticed his coworker had gone quiet in the meeting. Everyone else kept talking. He waited until after, then asked her if everything was okay. It turned out she'd gotten bad news that morning and just needed someone to notice.

That kind of noticing is emotional intelligence. It's not about being calm all the time or never getting upset. It's about reading what's happening, in yourself and in others, and responding in a way that actually helps.

This quiz looks at how you handle six everyday moments: conflict, criticism, other people's moods, and your own reactions. Answer based on what you'd actually do, not what sounds best. Six questions, two minutes, one score.

## What your result means

**Still tuning in.** Emotions, yours and other people's, aren't always easy for you to read in the moment. You might notice a mood shift after the fact, once you've had time to think it over. This isn't a flaw. Reading emotions is a skill, and skills get sharper with practice and attention.

**Steady and aware.** You pick up on most emotional cues and generally respond well. You're not perfect at it, and sometimes a strong reaction catches you off guard. But most days, you notice what's going on around you and adjust accordingly.

**Highly attuned.** You read rooms quickly and tend to know what someone needs before they say it. That's a real strength in relationships and at work. The one thing to watch is absorbing too much of what other people feel and forgetting to check in on your own.

Wherever you landed, this is a snapshot, not a fixed score. Sleep, stress, and how much attention you're paying all shift it day to day.
