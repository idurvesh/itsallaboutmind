Meera was cooking dinner when her phone lit up with a message from her manager. "Call me when you get a chance." Her hands kept moving, cutting vegetables, stirring the pot, but her mind had already jumped three steps ahead.

That gap between what's actually happening and how your body reacts is your stress response. Some people barely notice a spike. Others feel it in their chest before they've even finished reading.

This quiz asks about the everyday moments where stress shows up: traffic, deadlines, feedback, small disappointments. Answer honestly, based on how you actually react, not how you think you should. Six questions, two minutes, one score.

## What your result means

**Reactive alarm.** Your stress response fires fast. Your body reacts before your mind has caught up with what's actually happening. This isn't a character flaw. It usually means your nervous system is quick to sound the alarm, even for smaller things. Noticing the gap between the trigger and the reaction is often the first useful step.

**Balanced responder.** This is the most common result. You handle normal pressure well and stay fairly steady day to day. The strain shows up when several things stack at once: a deadline, a disagreement, and a late night all in the same day. One at a time, you're fine. Together, they add up.

**Steady under pressure.** You rarely spike, and when you do, you recover quickly. That doesn't mean you don't feel stress. It means your system tends to settle faster than most. It's a genuine strength, though it can sometimes mean you underestimate how much you're actually carrying.

Wherever you landed, this is a snapshot of how you tend to react, not a fixed label. Sleep, workload, and how supported you feel all shift it over time.
