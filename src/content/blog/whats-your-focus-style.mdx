Priya sat down to write a report at 10 am. By 10:15 she had checked her phone twice, opened four browser tabs, and started an email she didn't finish. She got the report done by 6 pm anyway. Somehow.

Everyone's focus works differently. Some people lock into one task for an hour straight. Others need short bursts, a bit of background noise, and permission to jump around. Neither way is wrong. The useful part is knowing which one you are, so you can work with it instead of fighting it.

This quiz looks at six everyday moments: notifications, deadlines, noise, and getting interrupted. Answer based on what you actually do, not what you think good focus is supposed to look like.

## What your result means

**The Scattered Sprinter.** Your focus tends to arrive in short, intense bursts rather than long stretches. Notifications, noise, and new tabs pull you off course easily, and getting back into a task takes real effort. This isn't a lack of discipline. It usually means your attention is wired for variety, and long unbroken stretches of one thing wear it down fast.

**The Steady Switcher.** You can hold a task for a good while, but interruptions still cost you something. A ping or a question knocks you out of rhythm for a minute or two before you find your place again. Most days this works fine. The main thing to watch is how many small interruptions you let stack up before they add up to a lot of lost time.

**The Deep Diver.** You can disappear into a task for long stretches without much effort, and time tends to pass faster than you expect. That's a real advantage for anything that needs sustained attention. The trade-off is that surfacing for interruptions, meetings, or quick switches can feel more jarring for you than it does for other people.

Wherever you landed, this is a snapshot of how you focus right now, not a permanent label. Sleep, stress, and how interesting the task is all shift the number on a given day.
