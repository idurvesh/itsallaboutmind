Ananya's team was stuck on a client brief that made no sense on paper. Half the room wanted to rework the numbers until the plan lined up cleanly. The other half wanted to scrap the brief and pitch three wild alternatives instead. Ananya just watched, trying to work out which side she actually belonged to.

That split is not really about who is smarter. It is about how each person's mind prefers to work a problem. Some people narrow things down until one answer survives. Others open things up until new options appear.

This brain quiz is not a diagnosis and it will not sort you into a box for life. It is a quick read on your default problem-solving style, the one you reach for before you've had time to think about it. Six questions, two minutes, one score.

## What your result means

**The Divergent Thinker.** You open a problem up before you narrow it down. Given a blank page, you'll generate five directions before committing to one, and you often spot an option nobody else considered. This makes you genuinely useful in early-stage brainstorms and creative work. The trade-off is that closing things out, picking one path and finishing it, can feel harder than starting.

**The Adaptive Thinker.** You don't have one fixed mode. You'll widen a problem when it needs fresh options and narrow it fast when it needs a decision. Most people land here, and it's a useful place to be, because you can borrow whichever style the moment calls for. The cost is that switching modes takes a bit more conscious effort than staying in one lane.

**The Convergent Thinker.** You narrow in. Given a messy problem, you look for the constraints first, then work toward the single answer that satisfies them. This makes you the person people trust to actually finish things and catch what breaks. It can also mean you settle on the first workable answer before other angles get a fair look.

None of these are fixed traits. They describe how you default under pressure, not a ceiling on how you can think. Most people shift toward the other style when the problem genuinely calls for it.
