Most people start their day by reaching for their phone. Within seconds, they've ingested news, notifications, and other people's opinions — and their nervous system is already in reactive mode before they've had a single conscious thought.

What if you started differently?

The first ten minutes of your day have an outsized effect on everything that follows. Your first emotional state of the day tends to persist. Your first dominant thought creates a lens through which you filter everything else.

Here's a simple morning meditation practice that costs ten minutes and returns far more.

## Why Morning Specifically

Your brain is in a theta wave state upon waking — the same deeply receptive state you enter in hypnosis or deep meditation. Your subconscious is especially open to new input. This is the ideal window to set your internal state intentionally, before the world sets it for you.

It's also the most consistent time for practice. Life has a way of filling up. If your meditation depends on "finding a calm moment" later in the day, it usually doesn't happen.

## The Practice: Step by Step

**Before you touch your phone, do this:**

### Minute 1-2: Just Breathe

Sit upright — on your bed, in a chair, on the floor. Close your eyes. Take three long, slow, deep breaths. Exhale fully. Let the exhale be longer than the inhale.

Don't try to do anything yet. Just arrive.

### Minute 3-5: Body Scan

Bring your attention to the top of your head and slowly move it downward — face, jaw, neck, shoulders, chest, arms, belly, legs. Wherever you feel tension, breathe into it. You're not trying to fix anything, just noticing.

This shifts your attention out of your head and into your body — where your actual feelings live.

### Minute 6-8: Gratitude and Intention

Ask yourself two questions and genuinely sit with the answers:

1. **What am I grateful for right now?** Don't list things automatically. Feel at least one of them fully — the warmth of your bed, someone you love, a simple fact of your health. Let gratitude become a felt experience, not a checklist.

2. **How do I want to show up today?** Not what you want to get done — how do you want to *be*? Calm? Focused? Patient? Open? Choose one quality and hold it clearly.

### Minute 9-10: Visualise One Thing

Spend sixty seconds imagining one thing going well today. A conversation, a task, a moment. See it clearly, feel the positive emotion of it working out. Let your body feel the good outcome before it's happened.

That's it.

## What to Expect

The first few days, your mind will wander constantly. That's normal — it's not failure, it's practice. Every time you notice you've drifted and return to the practice, you're training the attention muscle.

After one week of consistency, mornings feel different. Less reactive, more grounded. You'll notice you handle friction better when it appears.

After one month, missing the practice will feel like leaving the house without something important.

## The One Rule

Do it before your phone. Non-negotiable. The entire value of this practice is that *you* set your mental state before the world attempts to set it for you.

Even on mornings when you have no time, do two minutes. Two minutes of intentional breathing beats zero minutes of meditation and ten minutes of scrolling every single time.
